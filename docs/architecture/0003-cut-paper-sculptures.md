# ADR 0003: editable cut-paper sculptures and valid creation

Status: implementation contract for 0.02.000; 0.01.000 remains preserved.

The original workshop exposed mostly rectangular and triangular mechanism diagrams. A primary add action could also append a V-fold beyond the remaining card capacity. The new experience must provide expressive creations, direct editing, reliable creation and a fixed viewport instrument.

Retain the independently checked parallel-step and symmetric V-fold kinematics. Add original procedural cut-paper profiles and apertures within each existing rigid panel. These remove material; they do not add unmodelled hinges, forces, attachments or collisions. Keep a protected material web around hinge segments, use the same panel profile for 3D triangulation and fabrication, and preserve the conservative parent-panel bounds for closure and motion-lane certificates. The certificate concerns the documented zero-thickness geometry; cut-paper strength and physical assembly remain untested.

Architectural arcades, botanical wings, ribbed leaves and lattice compositions are editable geometry, not images or replacement colors. The original six learning projects remain available. Additional multi-part scenes provide useful fabrication starting points. Imports without cutwork preserve their exact legacy geometry. Every cutwork parameter is bounded and validated; exports retain actual cut lines and original crease positions.

Creation searches real free motion intervals and chooses dimensions that fit before insertion, preserving all existing parts and pins. When capacity is unavailable the project remains unchanged. The primary controls explain available capacity rather than manufacture an invalid default.

Selected parts expose projected manipulation handles on the 3D canvas. Parallel steps expose gutter position, rise, depth and width; V inserts expose gutter position, wing span and ridge length. A gesture captures its initial project and previews candidate dimensions without saving. Pointer release creates one undoable change. Escape cancels. Touch and keyboard use the same transaction. Orbit controls pause during manipulation. A bounded one-parameter search clips requested changes to the supported motion, closure, tab and label domain; pinned dimensions remain unchanged. This constraint is visible beside the handle rather than blanking the model. Canvas creation, deletion and profile selection use the same project operations as the inspector.

Cutwork is an optional schema-v1 extension: pattern (`arcade`, `leaf`, `wing`, `lattice`), integer detail 1–6 and protected web 1–5 mm. Closed apertures stay at least the selected web from every outline segment, using exact segment distances. Disjoint cell boxes retain at least twice the selected web between apertures. Triangular leaf/wing free edges may be trimmed inward, retaining both original hinges and their endpoints. The original straight outside cut is retained as an offcut boundary in fabrication; the assembly guide tells the maker to discard the strip. No decorative label is printed over a cutwork opening. Matching tab labels remain intact.

All material triangles, preview holes and cut-line loops come from `core/cutwork.ts`. A bounded immutable profile cache avoids recomputing planar triangulation while the card opens. Parent-panel bounds remain conservative because every retained point is inside its original rigid face. This is not a strength or manufacturability certificate for arbitrary card stock. The FOLD export continues to be a flat cut/crease graph with no claim about glued topology or material faces.

The product identity is deliberately separate from the earlier apps: warm paper, cobalt controls and orange manipulation accents, with locally served Space Grotesk and IBM Plex Sans. Unmodified upstream fonts, complete OFL notices and pinned source hashes are distributed together. Runtime font requests remain on the app origin.

The workbench uses the available viewport with a dominant persistent scene and internally scrolling controls. Responsive layouts preserve simultaneous visual feedback while keeping every operation reachable. Camera framing follows actual visible bounds and available stage dimensions.

Research: [Li et al. 2011](https://cg.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/people/~xianying/Papers/V-Popup/) studies a broader class of rigid V-style pop-ups; Plega does not claim its full construction algorithm. [Robert Sabuda's original instructional site](https://www.robertsabuda.com/butterfly-beginner.html) illustrates the useful connection between expressive shapes, score/cut instructions and matched glue locations. No third-party pattern, artwork or text is copied. Plega's profiles and projects are authored independently.
